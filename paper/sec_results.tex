\section{Rollouts relax toward a conditioning-set default}\label{sec:relax}

\paragraph{Tone.} Observationally, the 31-s change of every global statistic is anti-correlated with its opening value
($r$ from $-0.79$ to $-0.95$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:attr}), but openings differ in content too, so we intervened: we
shifted the brightness and colour temperature of the same opening within each of 24 scenes (12 NAVSIM, 12 generated)
and continued every version with the generic prompt (study 6, 31\,s; study 10, 60\,s). The versions converge
(Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). At 60\,s every cache configuration has relaxed (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}a): $\alpha$ ranges from
$-0.01$ (LongLive without a sink) to 0.79 (Self Forcing GAN), ranked as at 31\,s (Spearman $\rho=0.78$, $p=0.008$).
Part of the loss is immediate: most configurations lose a fifth to a third of the shift within 4.5\,s, and SiD loses
two thirds there and nothing afterwards (0.66 by 4.5\,s, 0.65 by 60\,s), which looks like rejecting an off-manifold
shift rather than forgetting it. Colour temperature behaves like brightness. Persistence varies by checkpoint even with architecture,
context and sampler fixed (Self Forcing: 0.35 to 0.79 at 60\,s; two SiD checkpoints differ by 0.36), so the training
objective alone does not set it.

\paragraph{Is the response linear in the shift?} Equation~\ref{eq:law} treats $\alpha$ as independent of how far the
opening was shifted, but every $\alpha$ above comes from one pair of shifts ($\pm16$~\Lstar). Study 23 shifted the same
24 openings by $-24$, $-8$, 0, $+8$ and $+24$~\Lstar{} (Self Forcing, 60\,s, two seeds, 720 videos) and tested
equivalence within $\pm0.15$. \pend{[results: large vs small shift with sink, re-rotated sink; darkening vs
brightening; no sink forgets a large shift too.]} In study 6, persistence from $\pm8$ and from $\pm16$ shifts agrees
within 0.09 in 12 of 14 configurations at 31\,s (exploratory; Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\subsection{Scene content}\label{sec:content}
Global tone is the easiest statistic to shift and measure, but it is not what a user sees as ``the scene''. We
therefore generated openings of six scene classes (snow, desert, forest, coast, mountain, farmland; four each, selected
by CLIP before any continuation) and continued each under the generic city prompt and under its own class prompt, with
eight configurations (study 9). Content relaxes like tone (Fig.~\ref{fig:content}). Under the city prompt, $\kappa$ at
30\,s ranges from 0.07 (SiD) to 0.61 (Rolling Forcing), and the opening's class is CLIP's top label in only 34\% of Self
Forcing frames at 30\,s. Under the opening's own class prompt the class persists ($\kappa$ 0.71--1.05; 9.3
\ci{8.1}{10.5} more log-odds than under the city prompt, preregistered), as a fresh rollout of that prompt would also
show it; the informative result is the loss under the city prompt. Content and tone persistence tend to go together across
configurations ($\rho=0.67$, $n=8$, $p=0.07$). Class scores could partly reflect colour (snow is bright, sand is
yellow), but scoring frames whose CIELAB mean and spread are matched to the opening's gives the same results, as does
SigLIP (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

Sinks act differently on content. Self Forcing's sink keeps more of it at 60\,s ($\kappa$ 0.61 vs 0.14, $+0.48$
\ci{0.39}{0.57}, preregistered), but not steadily: $\kappa$ falls to 0.32 at 30\,s and rises again, and the dunes, the
sea and the mountains of the opening reappear after a minute of city (Fig.~\ref{fig:content}, left). The returns are
positional (study 16, 24 openings): with the sink's keys re-rotated to sit just before the window, they vanish (burst
contrast $-0.29$ \ci{-0.37}{-0.22}), and the content kept at 60\,s falls from 0.56 to 0.25 ($-0.31$
\ci{-0.37}{-0.25}). With this one seed the re-rotated sink kept no detectably more content than no sink ($+0.10$
\ci{-0.00}{0.21}, not supported). Three new seeds per opening (study 21, 240 videos) reproduce the sink's advantage,
the loss under re-rotation ($-0.40$ \ci{-0.47}{-0.34}) and the positional return, and, averaged per opening, detect a
small share kept by the re-rotated sink: 0.19 against 0.11 without a sink ($+0.08$, Holm $p=0.006$; \ci{-0.01}{0.16}
when the fresh starts are also resampled). What a Self Forcing sink keeps of the scene, it keeps mostly by periodically
re-injecting the opening. LongLive's sink, which keeps tone, does not keep content (0.22 vs 0.24, preregistered test not
supported).

Content relaxes outside the Wan-1.3B family too. FramePack, continuing the same 24 openings under the city prompt,
keeps 0.55 of the class evidence at 20\,s (Self Forcing: 0.40 with a sink, 0.31 without; study 17). Giving its two
routes different images separates them (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:framepack}b): the clean context prefix alone keeps 0.24
\ci{0.12}{0.37}, levelling off after 14\,s, while the SigLIP image embedding alone, with a city frame in the context,
\emph{pulls} the video toward the opening's class, to 0.23 \ci{0.16}{0.30} at 20\,s and still rising. The same
embedding has no effect on tone (study 11), so in FramePack it sets the default of the scene but not of its
brightness. Tone and content can also part the other way: Krea Realtime 14B's first-frame anchor keeps the
opening's class ($\kappa$ 0.37 vs 0.05 without it at 60\,s, study 22; SigLIP and tone-normalised scores agree) but none
of its tone (Section~\ref{sec:mech}). \pend{[MAGI-1 (study 19) content results.]}

\subsection{The default}\label{sec:default}
\paragraph{The conditioning sets it.} Continuing the same 40 openings under four lighting prompts gives four defaults:
on Self Forcing, sunny 43, overcast 34, dusk 19 and night 16~\Lstar; on LongLive the same words give 45, 40, 32 and 21
(Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:default}). The mapping from words to brightness is model-specific. Under one prompt the default
also differs between checkpoints of one architecture (zero-drift levels from 17 to 54~\Lstar), and it tracks where
prompt-only rollouts end ($r=0.93$, slope 1.58; Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{Persistence depends on the scene.} Equation~\ref{eq:law} separates a model term ($\alpha$) from a prompt
term ($\mu$), but $\alpha$ itself varies: Self Forcing keeps 0.34 of the opening under a sunny prompt and 0.05 under a
night prompt (exploratory interaction, $p=0.001$). In four new scene types with their own prompts and self-generated
openings (a park walk, a drone flight over a coast, a kitchen, a market; study 12), its persistence at 60\,s differs
between them (0.44 to 0.67, $p=0.049$, preregistered), as does LongLive's at 30\,s (0.10 to 0.43). The sink effect
replicates almost exactly outside driving scenes: 0.55 with a sink and 0.02 without ($+0.53$ \ci{0.45}{0.61}).

\paragraph{Two components of drift.} For a prompt-only rollout, Eq.~\ref{eq:law} says nothing about why the default
should differ from where the same prompt starts. It does, and by how much depends on training (Appendix
Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}a). Fresh rollouts of Self Forcing (trained on its own 5-s rollouts; a checkpoint trained on 10-s rollouts
alike) and CausVid end 4--11~\Lstar{} from where they start; those of LongLive and Rolling Forcing, trained or tuned on their own long rollouts, 0.7--2.3~\Lstar.
Yet LongLive still forgets its opening ($\alpha=0.02$ without a sink at 31\,s). In this exploratory analysis drift has
two separable parts: \emph{forgetting} the opening, $(1-\alpha)(x_{\text{open}}-\mu)$, which every attention-based
model does, and an \emph{attractor offset} $\mu - x_{\text{start}}$, which is near zero in the two models trained on
their own long rollouts (other differences between them and the short-clip models cannot be excluded). Two later
models fit this pattern: fresh rollouts of Krea 14B, distilled with Self Forcing, brighten by 4.8~\Lstar{} over
31\,s (8 seeds), and those of MAGI-1, trained chunk by chunk on real videos rather than on its own rollouts, by 15--20~\Lstar{} over
30\,s. Prompt-only drift metrics mix the two, and both keep rising over 31\,s even with a sink
(Appendix~\ref{app:ctx}); relaxation describes the memory of the opening, not the absence of degradation.

\section{What decides how much of the opening is kept}\label{sec:mech}
Figure~\ref{fig:hold} collects every configuration we ran for 60\,s, grouped by whether the opening stays in the
model's context. Once it leaves, it is forgotten; while it stays, a share is kept whose size depends on the model; and
sinks left at their absolute rotary positions add bursts in which the opening returns.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_hold.pdf}
\caption{Whether the opening stays in the context decides how much of it is kept. (a) Persistence of an \Lstar{} shift
at 60\,s (window [50, 60)\,s; MAGI-1 full history: 30\,s) with scene-bootstrap 95\% CIs; ticks: at 10\,s. (b)
Persistence over time (10-s windows) for one or two configurations per group.}
\label{fig:hold}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Once the opening leaves the context, it is forgotten.} Without a sink, Self Forcing and LongLive keep 0.05
and $-0.01$ at 60\,s (study 10). MAGI-1~\cite{magi} (4.5B, distilled), which shares neither architecture nor training
with the other models, attends in its released configuration to the four previous 1-s chunks, so the opening leaves
its context after about 4\,s; persistence falls from 0.87 at 5\,s to 0.34 at 30\,s ($-0.52$ \ci{-0.70}{-0.35}, study
18) \pend{and to [x] at 60\,s (study 19)}. Krea Realtime 14B~\cite{krea}, a Self Forcing student of Wan2.1-14B,
recomputes its cache over a window of three latent frames (study 22, 24 scenes): it keeps 0.33 at 10\,s and nothing
from 20\,s on ($-0.05$ at 60\,s; $-0.38$ \ci{-0.45}{-0.31} from 10 to 60\,s), and its class evidence falls to $\kappa=0.05$.
The pattern holds at 14B parameters, although Krea's window also differs, so this is not a controlled test of scale. Re-denoising overlapping frames delays forgetting but does not stop it.
SkyReels-V2-DF extends videos in 97-frame segments that re-denoise the last 17 frames of the previous segment jointly
with the new ones. It holds the opening for 20\,s ($\alpha = 0.96$ \ci{0.86}{1.07}, whatever its guidance, history
noise or number of steps, exploratory; with a 5-frame overlap it relaxes, $-0.24$ \ci{-0.40}{-0.09} at 15--20\,s,
preregistered) and then forgets steadily, to 0.29 at 60\,s ($-0.72$ \ci{-0.81}{-0.63} from 20 to 60\,s; 5 frames: 0.08;
study 16).

\paragraph{An opening that stays in the context keeps a share.} Self Forcing keeps the sink's keys at their absolute
rotary positions, ever further from the current chunk. Re-rotated to sit just before the window, as in Rolling Forcing
and Deep Forcing, the sink stays at query distances seen in training. Re-rotated sinks of three and six frames keep
the same share at 60\,s (0.23 and 0.22, $-0.00$ \ci{-0.11}{0.10}, study 16; the preregistered depth test is not
supported) and one frame keeps 0.08 (study 10); with three new seeds per scene a three-frame sink keeps 0.21 against
$-0.01$ without one ($+0.22$ \ci{0.15}{0.29}, study 20, in each seed alone too). Rolling Forcing, which re-aligns its
anchor and was trained on longer rollouts, keeps 0.45. Krea's optional first-frame anchor, one latent frame next to its
window, keeps scene content but not tone: $\kappa=0.37$ against 0.05 without it ($+0.33$ \ci{0.18}{0.47}, preregistered;
the opening's class is CLIP's top label in 37\% of frames against 14\%), but $\alpha=0.03$ \ci{-0.03}{0.08} (both tone
tests not supported), close to the 0.08 of a one-frame re-rotated sink in Self Forcing. FramePack~\cite{framepack} (13B, fine-tuned from HunyuanVideo~\cite{hunyuan})
places the opening as a clean prefix frame in the context of \emph{every} 33-frame section and also feeds its SigLIP
embedding as a global condition. The shift travels through the prefix: sent only through the prefix it keeps about
half at 20\,s, only through the embedding nothing (0.01 \ci{-0.14}{0.17}, study 11); at 60\,s FramePack keeps 0.51
\ci{0.29}{0.71}, and 0.46 \ci{0.28}{0.65} with the prefix alone (study 16). MAGI-1 with full history keeps 0.64
\ci{0.45}{0.83} at 30\,s ($+0.30$ \ci{0.17}{0.43} over the released window, study 18) \pend{and [x] at 60\,s}. These
shares are still falling at 60\,s, slowly: from 30 to 60\,s the re-rotated sink goes from 0.36 to 0.21 (study 20), Rolling
Forcing from 0.59 to 0.45 and FramePack from 0.66 to 0.51. \pend{[Study 24, 180\,s: whether they level off.]} Keeping the
opening does not stop the drift the openings share: MAGI-1's fresh rollouts brighten by 15--20~\Lstar{} under either
setting, so full history preserves the difference between openings, not their level.

\paragraph{Sinks at their absolute positions: bursts.} Without re-rotation a sink keeps more, and depth helps: no sink,
one, three and six frames keep 0.05, 0.29, 0.61 and 0.76 at 60\,s ($+0.15$ \ci{0.11}{0.20} for six vs three; studies 7,
10), and LongLive's sink 0.45 against $-0.01$. Much of this is positional. Persistence jumps near 17, 33 and 51\,s in
every configuration with a sink at its absolute positions, across five Self Forcing checkpoints and LongLive (Appendix
Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}b, c), never with a re-rotated sink or without one, and the opening's scene returns at these times
(Section~\ref{sec:content}). Re-rotation lowers persistence at 60\,s from 0.61 to 0.16 ($-0.44$ \ci{-0.54}{-0.35},
preregistered), and by $-0.27$ \ci{-0.38}{-0.16} between the bursts (40--50\,s). An attention probe locates the bursts
(exploratory; Fig.~\ref{fig:probe}a): for the queries of a chunk we computed the share of attention the sink would
receive if its keys sat at each query--sink distance (4 openings, 20 chunks each, all layers and denoising passes). The
share peaks sharply at distances 71, 134, 203 and 266 latent frames (0.11--0.15 against a median of 0.07), identically
in every chunk and opening, so the peaks belong to the positional code, not to content; a sink at its original
positions passes the first three 16.5, 32.3 and 49.5\,s after the opening, the times of the bursts. Moving the sink
moves the bursts (study 25, preregistered; Fig.~\ref{fig:probe}b): 18 or 36 latent frames further back, the probe puts
every burst 4.5 or 9\,s earlier, and persistence rose in the windows predicted for the moved sink rather than in the
original ones (difference in differences $+0.53$ \ci{0.45}{0.61} and $+0.25$ \ci{0.21}{0.30}). A sink held at one
distance does not burst, and the distance sets how much is kept, in the order of the probe's shares
(Fig.~\ref{fig:probe}c): 0.86 at 60\,s at the peak distance 203 (share 0.150), 0.22 next to the window (0.079) and
0.01 at the trough before the peak (0.052; peak $-$ trough $+0.85$ \ci{0.78}{0.91}, peak $-$ window $+0.64$
\ci{0.54}{0.74}). Held at the peak, though, the video freezes on its opening (frame-to-frame change 1.1 grey levels
over 50--60\,s, against 2.4 with the sink in place; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s25frames}). A burst is thus a brief copy
of the opening while the sink's distance passes a peak. A second probe on four other openings, to 723 frames
(180\,s), reproduces the curve ($r=0.999$) and, by a peak rule fixed before its output was read, finds 13 further
peaks after 60\,s, preregistered as burst times for the 180-s videos of study 24 (study 26). \pend{[Study 26: bursts
at the 13 predicted onsets, contrast [x] (Self Forcing) and [x] (LongLive), [x] more than at control onsets.]}
LoL~\cite{lol} relates a similar failure, ``sink collapse'', to rotary phases~\cite{riflex,roundround}.

\paragraph{An action-conditioned world model.} Matrix-Game-2.0~\cite{matrixgame2}, driven by keyboard and mouse
actions with no text, fits the same picture (study 15): a shift held only in its 6-frame context fades ($-0.24$
\ci{-0.35}{-0.14} from 5 to 30\,s, preregistered; 7 scenes, as the model often collapses to black), while a shift sent
only through its CLIP image embedding, which conditions every chunk, persists (0.43 \ci{0.13}{0.71} at 30\,s, Holm
$p=0.075$; 0.25 \ci{0.17}{0.36} at 15\,s over 20 scenes, exploratory; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:framepack}d).

\paragraph{A phenomenological recursion.} A linear recursion of a chunk's mean statistic on the window, the sink and the
prompt's default describes the first 20\,s of these curves. Its prompt weight, $w_p\approx0.08$ from a fit to 120-s
curves, matched a bit-exact cache-replay measurement that swaps only the text conditioning for one chunk (0.088
\ci{0.045}{0.149} at 15\,s, study 14). It does not hold beyond 20\,s and explains neither the share of re-rotated sinks
nor the bursts (Appendix~\ref{app:recursion}).
