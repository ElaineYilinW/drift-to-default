\section{Rollouts relax toward a conditioning-set default}\label{sec:relax}

\paragraph{Tone.} Observationally, the 31-s change of every global statistic is anti-correlated with its opening value
($r$ from $-0.79$ to $-0.95$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:attr}), but openings differ in content too, so we intervened: we
shifted the brightness and colour temperature of the same opening within each of 24 scenes (12 NAVSIM, 12 generated)
and continued every version with the generic prompt (study 6, 31\,s; study 10, 60\,s). The versions converge
(Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). At 60\,s every cache configuration has relaxed (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}a): $\alpha$ ranges from
$-0.01$ (LongLive without a sink) to 0.79 (Self Forcing GAN), ranked as at 31\,s (Spearman $\rho=0.78$, $p=0.008$).
Colour temperature behaves like brightness, the response is linear in the shift from $-24$ to $+24$~\Lstar{} (study
23), and high persistence is not a frozen video (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). Persistence varies by checkpoint even with architecture,
context and sampler fixed (Self Forcing: 0.35 to 0.79 at 60\,s; two SiD checkpoints differ by 0.36), so the training
objective alone does not set it.

\subsection{Scene content}\label{sec:content}
Tone is not what a user sees as ``the scene''. We generated openings of six scene classes (snow, desert, forest, coast, mountain, farmland; four each, selected
by CLIP before any continuation) and continued each under the generic city prompt and under its own class prompt, with
eight configurations (study 9). Content relaxes like tone (Fig.~\ref{fig:content}). Under the city prompt, $\kappa$ at
30\,s ranges from 0.07 (SiD) to 0.61 (Rolling Forcing), and the opening's class is CLIP's top label in only 34\% of Self
Forcing frames at 30\,s. Under the opening's own class prompt the class persists ($\kappa$ 0.71--1.05; 9.3
\ci{8.1}{10.5} more log-odds, preregistered), as a fresh rollout of that prompt would. Across the eight
configurations, content and tone persistence are correlated, but not significantly ($\rho=0.67$, $p=0.07$). Scoring frames whose CIELAB mean and spread are matched to the
opening's, or scoring with SigLIP or DINOv2, gives the same results (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

Sinks act differently on content. Self Forcing's sink keeps more of it at 60\,s ($\kappa$ 0.61 vs 0.14, $+0.48$
\ci{0.39}{0.57}, preregistered), but not steadily: $\kappa$ falls to 0.32 at 30\,s and rises again, and the dunes, the
sea and the mountains of the opening reappear after a minute of city (Fig.~\ref{fig:content}, left). The returns are
positional (study 16, 24 openings): with the sink's keys re-rotated to sit just before the window, they vanish (burst
contrast, $\kappa$ over 50--55\,s minus 45--50\,s: $-0.29$ \ci{-0.37}{-0.22}), and the content kept at 60\,s falls from 0.56 to 0.25 ($-0.31$
\ci{-0.37}{-0.25}). Three new seeds per opening (study 21) reproduce the sink's advantage, the loss under re-rotation ($-0.40$
\ci{-0.47}{-0.34}) and the return, and suggest a small share kept by the re-rotated sink: 0.19 against 0.11 without a
sink ($+0.08$, Holm $p=0.006$, though an interval that also resamples the fresh starts is \ci{-0.01}{0.16}).
LongLive's sink, which keeps tone, does not keep content (0.22 vs 0.24, preregistered test not
supported).

Content relaxes outside the Wan-1.3B family too. FramePack, continuing the same 24 openings under the city prompt,
keeps 0.55 of the class evidence at 20\,s (Self Forcing: 0.40 with a sink, 0.31 without; study 17). Giving its two
routes different images separates them (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:framepack}b): the clean context prefix alone keeps 0.24
\ci{0.12}{0.37}, levelling off after 14\,s, while the SigLIP image embedding alone, with a city frame in the context,
\emph{pulls} the video toward the opening's class, to 0.23 \ci{0.16}{0.30} at 20\,s and still rising. The same
embedding has no effect on tone (study 11), so in FramePack it sets the default of the scene but not of its
brightness. Tone and content can also part the other way: Krea Realtime 14B's first-frame anchor keeps the
opening's class ($\kappa$ 0.37 vs 0.05 without it at 60\,s, $+0.33$ \ci{0.18}{0.47}, study 22; the class is CLIP's top
label in 37\% of frames against 14\%) but none of its tone (Section~\ref{sec:mech}). MAGI-1 behaves like the Wan models (study 19, preregistered): under the city prompt its class score falls by 10.7 \ci{9.4}{11.8} by 25--30\,s, its own class prompt sets a different default ($+7.3$ \ci{5.8}{8.8}), and full history keeps $\kappa=0.43$ against 0.29 with the released window ($+0.14$ \ci{-0.01}{0.30}, not supported).

\subsection{The default}\label{sec:default}
\paragraph{The conditioning sets it.} Continuing the same 40 openings under four lighting prompts gives four defaults:
on Self Forcing, sunny 43, overcast 34, dusk 19 and night 16~\Lstar; on LongLive the same words give 45, 40, 32 and 21
(Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:default}). Under one prompt the default
also differs between checkpoints of one architecture (zero-drift levels from 17 to 54~\Lstar), and it tracks where
prompt-only rollouts end ($r=0.93$, slope 1.58; Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). Regenerating one chunk from a bit-exact
copy of the cache with only its text conditioning swapped measures the prompt's pull per chunk: 0.088
\ci{0.045}{0.149} at 15\,s (study 14), as a linear recursion fitted to 120-s curves had predicted
(Appendix~\ref{app:recursion}).

\paragraph{Persistence depends on the scene.} Equation~\ref{eq:law} separates a model term ($\alpha$) from a prompt
term ($\mu$), but $\alpha$ itself varies: Self Forcing keeps 0.34 of the opening under a sunny prompt and 0.05 under a
night prompt (exploratory interaction, $p=0.001$). In four new scene types with their own prompts and self-generated
openings (study 12), its persistence at 60\,s differs
between them (0.44 to 0.67, $p=0.049$, preregistered; not significant across all tests), as does LongLive's at 30\,s (0.10 to 0.43). The sink effect
replicates almost exactly outside driving scenes: 0.55 with a sink and 0.02 without ($+0.53$ \ci{0.45}{0.61}).

\paragraph{Two components of drift.} For a prompt-only rollout, Eq.~\ref{eq:law} says nothing about why the default
should differ from where the same prompt starts. It does, and by how much depends on the model (Appendix
Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}a). Over 40 prompts (study 30, preregistered), fresh 60-s rollouts of Self Forcing without a sink end 16.4~\Lstar{} from where they start (mean $|$end$-$start$|$) and those of CausVid 11.7,
against 6.8 for LongLive, tuned from Self Forcing on its own long rollouts ($+9.6$ \ci{6.6}{12.4}, $+4.9$
\ci{2.0}{7.7}); with re-aligned sinks Self Forcing ends 9.6 away and Rolling Forcing 3.3 ($+6.3$ \ci{4.2}{8.6}). Yet
LongLive still forgets its opening ($\alpha=-0.01$ without a sink at 60\,s). Drift thus has two separable parts:
\emph{forgetting} the opening, $(1-\alpha)(x_{\text{open}}-\mu)$, which every attention-based model does, and an
\emph{attractor offset} $\mu - x_{\text{start}}$. The offset is set less by training than we expected: with
LongLive's 12-frame window instead of its 21, Self Forcing ends 8.0~\Lstar{} away ($-8.3$ \ci{-10.1}{-6.5}), no longer
significantly further than LongLive ($+1.3$ \ci{-1.0}{3.6}, study 32, preregistered), though it still darkens on
average ($-7.3$ against $-0.4$, exploratory). A sink at its absolute positions, which keeps returning to the opening,
hides these differences (3.8 vs 3.0~\Lstar). Fresh rollouts of Krea 14B brighten by about 4~\Lstar{} in 30\,s, those of
MAGI-1 by 15--20. Prompt-only drift metrics mix the two parts
(Appendix~\ref{app:ctx}); relaxation describes the memory of the opening, not the absence of degradation.

\section{What decides how much of the opening is kept}\label{sec:mech}
Figure~\ref{fig:hold} collects every configuration we ran for 60\,s, grouped by whether the opening stays in the
model's context. Once it leaves, it is forgotten; while it stays, a share is kept whose size depends on the model; and
sinks left at their absolute rotary positions add bursts in which the opening returns.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_hold.pdf}
\caption{Whether the opening stays in the context decides how much of it is kept. (a) Persistence of an \Lstar{} shift
at 60\,s (window [50, 60)\,s; MAGI-1 full history: 30\,s) with scene-bootstrap 95\% CIs; ticks: at 10\,s. (b)
Persistence over time (10-s windows) for one or two configurations per group.}
\label{fig:hold}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Once the opening leaves the context, it is forgotten.} Without a sink, Self Forcing and LongLive keep 0.05
and $-0.01$ at 60\,s (study 10). MAGI-1~\cite{magi} (4.5B, distilled; another architecture) attends in its released
configuration to the four previous 1-s chunks, so the opening leaves its context after about 4\,s; persistence falls from 0.87 at 5\,s to 0.34 at 30\,s ($-0.52$ \ci{-0.70}{-0.35}, study
18), and to 0.05 \ci{-0.10}{0.20} at 60\,s (study 19, 12 scenes; from 0.21 at 30\,s, a fall of $-0.17$ \ci{-0.41}{0.08} that is not significant). Krea Realtime 14B~\cite{krea}, a Self Forcing student of Wan2.1-14B,
recomputes its cache over a window of three latent frames (study 22, 24 scenes): it keeps 0.33 at 10\,s and nothing
from 20\,s on ($-0.05$ at 60\,s; $-0.38$ \ci{-0.45}{-0.31} from 10 to 60\,s), and its class evidence falls to $\kappa=0.05$.
The pattern holds at 14B, though Krea's window also differs. Re-denoising overlapping frames delays forgetting but does not stop it.
SkyReels-V2-DF, which re-denoises the last 17 frames of each 97-frame segment with the next, holds the opening for
20\,s ($\alpha = 0.96$ \ci{0.86}{1.07}, study 8; 1.00 in study 16) and then forgets, to 0.29 at 60\,s (study 16; with a 5-frame overlap, 0.08;
Appendix~\ref{app:other}).

\paragraph{An opening that stays in the context keeps a share of its tone, its scene or both.} Self Forcing keeps the sink's keys at their absolute
rotary positions, ever further from the current chunk. Re-rotated to sit just before the window, as in Rolling Forcing
and Deep Forcing, the sink stays at query distances seen in training. Re-rotated sinks of three and six frames keep
the same share at 60\,s (0.23 and 0.22, $-0.00$ \ci{-0.11}{0.10}, study 16; the preregistered depth test is not
supported) and one frame keeps 0.08 (study 10); with three new seeds per scene a three-frame sink keeps 0.21 against
$-0.01$ without one ($+0.22$ \ci{0.15}{0.29}, study 20). Rolling Forcing, which re-aligns its
anchor and was trained on longer rollouts, keeps 0.45. Krea's optional first-frame anchor, one latent frame next to its
window, keeps the scene (Section~\ref{sec:content}) but not tone: $\alpha=0.03$ \ci{-0.03}{0.08} (both tone tests not
supported), close to the 0.08 of a one-frame re-rotated sink in Self Forcing. FramePack~\cite{framepack} (13B, fine-tuned from HunyuanVideo~\cite{hunyuan})
places the opening as a clean prefix frame in the context of \emph{every} 33-frame section and also feeds its SigLIP
embedding as a global condition. The shift travels through the prefix: sent only through the prefix it keeps about
half at 20\,s, only through the embedding nothing (0.01 \ci{-0.14}{0.17}, study 11); at 60\,s FramePack keeps 0.51
\ci{0.29}{0.71}, and 0.46 \ci{0.28}{0.65} with the prefix alone (study 16), partly in videos that stopped moving
(Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). MAGI-1 with full history keeps 0.64
\ci{0.45}{0.83} at 30\,s ($+0.30$ \ci{0.17}{0.43} over the released window, study 18). These
shares are still falling at 60\,s. At 180\,s (study 24, preregistered) the re-rotated sink still keeps 0.12
\ci{0.04}{0.20} and Rolling Forcing 0.29 \ci{0.21}{0.37}; on new seeds to 240\,s, over 60-s windows (study 29,
preregistered), Rolling Forcing's share is steady from the second minute (0.32, 0.29, 0.32; change $+0.01$ \ci{-0.04}{0.06}, equivalent within $\pm0.10$), the re-rotated sink
still keeps 0.10 \ci{0.06}{0.14} in the fourth minute but is not shown steady (0.18, 0.18, 0.10; one of two seeds
falls), and without a sink the opening stays forgotten (0.00 \ci{-0.05}{0.05}). Keeping the
opening does not stop the drift the openings share: MAGI-1's fresh rollouts brighten by 15--20~\Lstar{} under either
setting, so full history preserves the difference between openings, not their level.

\paragraph{Sinks at their absolute positions: bursts.} Without re-rotation a sink keeps more, and depth helps: no sink,
one, three and six frames keep 0.05, 0.29, 0.61 and 0.76 at 60\,s ($+0.15$ \ci{0.11}{0.20} for six vs three; studies 7,
10), and LongLive's sink 0.45 against $-0.01$. Much of this is positional. Persistence jumps near 17, 33 and 51\,s in
every configuration with a sink of three or more frames at its absolute positions, across five Self Forcing checkpoints and LongLive (Appendix
Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}b, c), never with a re-rotated sink or without one, and the opening's scene returns at these times
(Section~\ref{sec:content}). Re-rotation lowers persistence at 60\,s from 0.61 to 0.16 ($-0.44$ \ci{-0.54}{-0.35},
preregistered), and by $-0.27$ \ci{-0.38}{-0.16} between the bursts (40--50\,s). An attention probe locates the bursts
(exploratory; Fig.~\ref{fig:probe}a): the share of attention a chunk's queries would give the sink if its keys sat at
each query--sink distance peaks sharply at 71, 134, 203 and 266 latent frames (0.11--0.17 against a median of 0.07),
identically in every chunk and opening, so the peaks are positional, not content; a sink at its original
positions passes the first three 16.5, 32.3 and 49.5\,s after the opening, the times of the bursts. Moving the sink
moves the bursts (study 25, preregistered; Fig.~\ref{fig:probe}b): 18 or 36 latent frames further back, the probe puts
every burst 4.5 or 9\,s earlier, and persistence rose in the windows predicted for the moved sink rather than in the
original ones (predicted minus original windows, net of the sink in place: $+0.53$ \ci{0.45}{0.61} and $+0.25$
\ci{0.21}{0.30}). A sink held at one
distance does not burst, and the probe's share at that distance sets how much is kept (Fig.~\ref{fig:probe}c): 0.86
at 60\,s at the peak distance 203 and 0.01 at the trough before it ($+0.85$ \ci{0.78}{0.91}, study 25), and over 14
further distances chosen by a rule fixed in advance, 9 of them beyond any distance a sink reaches within 60\,s,
persistence rose with the share (mean within-scene Spearman $\rho=+0.83$ \ci{0.79}{0.86}, study 27, preregistered),
also among the 7 lowest shares ($+0.35$ \ci{0.20}{0.49}). The price is motion ($\rho=-0.81$ \ci{-0.84}{-0.79}):
every distance with a share of 0.11 or more froze the video on its opening (persistence 0.78--0.87, frame-to-frame
change 0.7--1.1 grey levels against 2.4 with the sink in place; Appendix Figs.~\ref{fig:s25frames},~\ref{fig:held}),
every distance below kept moving, and the change was abrupt (exploratory). So does LongLive (study 28, preregistered): moving its sink
moves its bursts ($+0.43$ and $+0.36$), and held, its persistence rises with its own probe's share ($\rho=+0.81$
\ci{0.78}{0.84}), correlating 0.94 with Self Forcing's across distances. A burst
is thus a brief copy of the opening while the sink's distance passes a peak. A second probe to 180\,s found 13 further peaks, which we preregistered
as burst times for the 180-s videos of study 24 (study 26; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:long}b): persistence jumped at these
onsets, by $+0.19$ \ci{0.17}{0.20} in Self Forcing ($+0.32$ \ci{0.29}{0.35} more than at control onsets between them)
and $+0.13$ \ci{0.12}{0.15} in LongLive, and 12 of the 13 large
jumps after 60\,s followed a predicted onset (chance 0.33).

\paragraph{A world model.} In Matrix-Game-2.0~\cite{matrixgame2}, driven by actions without text, a shift held only in
its 6-frame context fades ($-0.24$ \ci{-0.35}{-0.14} from 5 to 30\,s, study 15, preregistered), while one sent through
its CLIP image embedding persists (0.25 at 15\,s, exploratory; Appendix~\ref{app:other}).
