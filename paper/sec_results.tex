\section{Rollouts relax toward a conditioning-set default}\label{sec:relax}

\paragraph{Tone.} Observationally, the 31-s change of every global statistic is anti-correlated with its opening value
($r$ from $-0.79$ to $-0.95$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:attr}), but openings differ in content too, so we intervened: we
shifted the brightness and colour temperature of the same opening within
each of 24 scenes (12 NAVSIM, 12 generated) and continued every version with the generic prompt (study 6, 31\,s;
study 10, 60\,s). Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} shows the result for Self Forcing: the versions converge, and an attention
sink keeps them apart by a fixed share. Figure~\ref{fig:atlas}a gives $\alpha$ at 60\,s for every configuration.
At 60\,s every cache configuration has relaxed: $\alpha$ ranges from $-0.01$ (LongLive without a sink) to 0.79
(Self Forcing GAN), and the ranking agrees with the one at 31\,s (Spearman $\rho=0.78$, $p=0.008$). Part of the loss is
immediate: most configurations lose a fifth to a third of the shift within 4.5\,s, and SiD loses almost all of its total there (0.66 of 0.65), which looks like the model rejecting an
off-manifold shift rather than forgetting it. Colour temperature behaves like brightness. Persistence varies by checkpoint even with architecture, context and
sampler fixed (Self Forcing: 0.35 to 0.79 at 60\,s; two SiD checkpoints differ by 0.36), so we attribute it to the
checkpoint rather than to the training objective.

\subsection{Scene content}\label{sec:content}
Global tone is the easiest statistic to shift and measure, but it is not what a user sees as ``the scene''. We
therefore generated openings of six scene classes (snow, desert, forest, coast, mountain, farmland; four openings each,
selected by CLIP before any continuation) and continued each under the generic city prompt and under its own class
prompt, with eight configurations (study 9). Content relaxes like tone (Fig.~\ref{fig:content}). Under the city
prompt, $\kappa$ at 30\,s ranges from 0.07 (SiD) to 0.61 (Rolling Forcing); six of eight configurations lose most of
the class evidence, and the opening's class is CLIP's top label in only 34\% of Self Forcing frames at 30\,s.
Under the opening's own class prompt the class persists ($\kappa$ 0.71--1.05), as a fresh rollout of that prompt would
also show it: the class prompt keeps 9.3 \ci{8.1}{10.5} more log-odds than the city prompt at 30\,s (preregistered; 9.0
after each frame's tone is matched to the opening's). The informative result is the loss under the city prompt.
Content persistence is correlated with tone persistence across configurations ($\rho=0.67$, $p=0.07$, $n=8$).

Sinks act differently on content. Self Forcing's sink keeps more of it at 60\,s ($\kappa$ 0.61 vs 0.14, $+0.48$
\ci{0.39}{0.57}, preregistered), but not steadily: $\kappa$ falls to 0.32 at 30\,s and rises again, and in the
frames the dunes, the sea and the mountains of the opening reappear after a minute of city (Fig.~\ref{fig:content},
left). The returns coincide with the positional bursts in tone persistence (Section~\ref{sec:mech}), and they are
positional too (study 16, 24 openings): with the sink's keys re-rotated to sit just before the window, the second
return vanishes (burst contrast $+0.31\to+0.01$, $-0.29$ \ci{-0.37}{-0.22}), the first as well ($+0.20\to-0.03$),
and the content kept at 60\,s falls from 0.56 to 0.25 ($-0.31$ \ci{-0.37}{-0.25}); both tests were preregistered and
hold on tone-normalised embeddings. A re-rotated sink keeps no detectably more content than no sink (0.25 vs 0.15,
$+0.10$ \ci{-0.00}{0.21}, preregistered test not supported); its content curve lies on the no-sink curve. What a
Self Forcing sink keeps of the scene, it keeps by periodically re-injecting the opening.
LongLive's sink, which keeps tone, does not keep content (0.22 vs 0.24, preregistered test not supported).

Content relaxes outside the Wan family too. FramePack, continuing the same 24 openings under the city prompt, keeps
0.55 of the class evidence at 20\,s (Self Forcing: 0.40 with a sink, 0.31 without), and the class score falls by 6.7
\ci{5.2}{8.3} log-odds (study 17, preregistered). We separated its two routes by giving the context prefix and the
SigLIP embedding different images, the opening or a generated city frame. The context prefix alone keeps 0.24
\ci{0.12}{0.37}, levelling off after 14\,s; the embedding alone, with a city frame in the context, starts at 0 and
\emph{pulls} the video toward the opening's class, to 0.23 \ci{0.16}{0.30} at 20\,s and still rising
(Fig.~\ref{fig:framepack}b); together they keep $+0.31$ \ci{0.20}{0.41} more than the context alone, about the sum of
the two. All four preregistered tests hold, also on tone-normalised embeddings. The same embedding has no effect on tone
(study 11), so in FramePack the image embedding sets the default of the scene but not of its brightness.

\subsection{The default}\label{sec:default}
\paragraph{The conditioning sets it.} Continuing the same 40 openings under four lighting prompts gives four defaults:
on Self Forcing, sunny 43, overcast 34, dusk 19 and night 16~\Lstar; on LongLive the same words give 45, 40, 32 and 21
(Appendix, Fig.~\ref{fig:default}). The prompt sets the target, and the mapping from words to brightness is model-specific. The
default also differs between checkpoints of one architecture under the same prompt (zero-drift levels from 17 to
54~\Lstar{} across configurations), and it tracks where prompt-only rollouts end ($r=0.93$, slope 1.58;
Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{Persistence is not independent of the prompt.} Equation~\ref{eq:law} separates a model term ($\alpha$) from
a prompt term ($\mu$), but $\alpha$ itself varies with the prompt: in the lighting experiment, Self Forcing keeps 0.34
of the opening under a sunny prompt and 0.05 under a night prompt (interaction $p=0.001$, exploratory; LongLive
$p=0.053$). We preregistered this test in four new scene types (a park walk, a drone flight over a coast, a kitchen, a
market; six self-generated openings each, study 12): Self Forcing's persistence at 60\,s differs between them (0.44 to
0.67, $p=0.049$), as does LongLive's at 30\,s (0.10 to 0.43, $p=0.009$, secondary). Because each scene type has its own
prompt and its own openings, this shows that persistence varies with the scene, not with the prompt alone. The sink effects replicate almost exactly
outside driving scenes: Self Forcing keeps 0.55 with a sink and 0.02 without at 60\,s ($+0.53$ \ci{0.45}{0.61}),
LongLive 0.24 vs 0.05 at 30\,s.

\paragraph{Two components of drift.} For a prompt-only rollout, Eq.~\ref{eq:law} says nothing about why the default
should differ from where the same prompt starts. It does, and by how much depends on training
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}a). For Self Forcing (5-s clips) and CausVid, fresh rollouts end 4--11~\Lstar{} from where
they start (Self Forcing darkens, CausVid brightens); for LongLive and Rolling Forcing, trained or tuned for long
rollouts, the offset is 0.7--2.3~\Lstar. Yet LongLive still forgets its opening ($\alpha=0.19$ with a sink, 0.02
without, at 31\,s). In this exploratory analysis drift has two separable parts: \emph{forgetting} the opening,
$(1-\alpha)(x_{\text{open}}-\mu)$, which every attention-based model does, and an \emph{attractor offset}
$\mu - x_{\text{start}}$, which is near zero in the two models trained for long rollouts (with only two such models, other
differences between them and the short-clip models cannot be excluded). Prompt-only drift metrics such as $D$ mix the two, and
both keep rising over 31\,s even with a sink (Appendix~\ref{app:ctx}); relaxation describes the memory of the opening,
not the absence of degradation.

\section{What decides how much of the opening is kept}\label{sec:mech}
\paragraph{Attention sinks keep a share.} In study 6, removing LongLive's sink lowered $\alpha$ at 31\,s from 0.19 to
0.02 ($-0.17$ \ci{-0.28}{-0.06}) and removing Self Forcing's lowered colour persistence from 0.25 to $-0.01$. For Self
Forcing brightness the preregistered test failed at 31\,s ($-0.06$ \ci{-0.17}{0.05}): without a sink Self Forcing
forgets slowly, so at 31\,s it had not yet forgotten (Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}b). At 60\,s (study 7) the sink keeps 0.58
of the opening and no sink 0.03 ($+0.55$ \ci{0.45}{0.65}); for CausVid 0.23 vs 0.04. Persistence grows with sink depth (study 10, 60\,s): no sink 0.05, one latent frame
0.29, three 0.61, six 0.76 ($+0.15$ \ci{0.11}{0.20} for six vs three; $+0.24$ \ci{0.14}{0.34} for one vs none, which
reverses an exploratory 31-s anomaly), and LongLive's sink keeps 0.45 against $-0.01$. The sink's \emph{position}
matters even more. Self Forcing keeps the sink keys at their absolute rotary positions, ever further from the current
chunk; re-rotating them to sit just before the window, as Rolling Forcing and Deep Forcing do, lowers persistence at
60\,s from 0.61 to 0.16 ($-0.44$ \ci{-0.54}{-0.35}, preregistered two-sided). In the window 40--50\,s, between the
bursts described below, the effect is smaller but clear ($-0.27$ \ci{-0.38}{-0.16}), and persistence still grows with
sink depth (0.08, 0.23, 0.48, 0.63). Depth, however, helps only at the original positions (study 16, 24 scenes):
re-rotation cuts a six-frame sink from 0.76 to 0.22 ($-0.54$ \ci{-0.63}{-0.46}), and re-rotated sinks of six and three
frames keep the same share (0.22 vs 0.23, $-0.00$ \ci{-0.11}{0.10}; preregistered test of a depth effect not
supported; 40--50\,s: $+0.03$ \ci{-0.09}{0.14}). Their curves coincide from 10\,s on. (The three-frame value is 0.16
in study 10 because 9 of the 24 scenes ran on another GPU architecture this time, which amounts to resampling them;
all 72 videos repeated on the same architecture were bit-identical.) A sink at in-distribution
positions thus holds a small, steady share of tone (about 0.2) whatever its depth, and almost none of the scene.

\paragraph{Re-conditioning on the opening keeps most of it.} SkyReels-V2-DF, which re-denoises the last 17 frames
of each segment jointly with the new ones, does not relax ($\alpha = 0.96$ \ci{0.86}{1.07} at 20\,s), whatever its
guidance, history noise or number of steps (exploratory). Cutting the overlap to 5 frames, with the same number of
extensions, makes it relax: $\alpha$ at 15--20\,s falls from 1.01 to 0.77 ($-0.24$ \ci{-0.40}{-0.09}, preregistered,
12 new scenes). With 17 frames, 5 of 12 scenes have $\alpha>1$: the overlap passes the opening forward and can amplify it.
FramePack~\cite{framepack}, a 13B model fine-tuned from HunyuanVideo~\cite{hunyuan}, places the opening as a clean
prefix frame in the context of \emph{every} 33-frame section and also feeds its SigLIP embedding as a global condition
(study 11, 12 scenes, 20\,s). It relaxes: 0.91 of a brightness shift at 5\,s and 0.68 \ci{0.57}{0.79} at 20\,s with
forward sampling (preregistered: more than half, supported). Over the same window that is as much as the best cache
configurations keep (six-frame sink 0.72, Rolling Forcing 0.64; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:matched}), not more. A shift
sent only through the context prefix levels off at about half (0.53) between 10 and 20\,s; FramePack's
anti-drifting sampling, which generates sections from the end back toward the opening, keeps 0.86. A shift sent only
through the image embedding does nothing (0.01 \ci{-0.14}{0.17}; preregistered test not supported): in FramePack the
image embedding does not carry tone. (We did not vary FramePack's text prompt.)

\paragraph{A model of its own architecture.} MAGI-1~\cite{magi} shares neither architecture nor training with the
other models, and its code lets us choose how long the opening stays in the context (study 18, 12 scenes, 30\,s). In
the released configuration, which attends to the four previous 1-s chunks, the opening leaves the context after about
4\,s; persistence is 0.87 at 5\,s and falls to 0.34 at 30\,s ($-0.52$ \ci{-0.70}{-0.35}) without levelling off
(Fig.~\ref{fig:framepack}c). With full history the curves coincide for the first 5\,s and then separate: persistence
levels off near 0.65 from 10\,s on (0.64 \ci{0.45}{0.83} at 30\,s, $+0.30$ \ci{0.17}{0.43} over the window). All three
preregistered tests hold, and none of the 48 videos froze. Keeping the opening in context does not stop the shared
drift: fresh rollouts brighten by 15--20~\Lstar{} under either setting, and the mean $|$end $-$ open$|$ is no smaller
with full history (13.2 vs 11.2~\Lstar). Full history preserves the difference between openings, not their level.

\paragraph{An action-conditioned world model.} Matrix-Game-2.0~\cite{matrixgame2} generates a world from an image and
keyboard and mouse actions, with no text. The opening's latent enters only the first chunk and leaves the 6-frame
key--value window after about a second (no sink); its CLIP~\cite{clip} embedding conditions every chunk. We continued
the 24 scenes with a constant forward action and sent the opening's brightness shift through both routes, the context
latent only, or the embedding only (study 15). The model often collapses to black: 40 of 144 videos turn black within
30\,s, mostly after 19\,s, so the preregistered 30-s analysis kept only 7 scenes. There, a
shift held only in the context faded ($-0.24$ \ci{-0.35}{-0.14}, supported), and the embedding-only shift persisted
(0.43 \ci{0.13}{0.71}) but did not pass the Holm-corrected threshold ($p=0.075$). At 15\,s, where 20 scenes remain
(exploratory), the picture is clear (Fig.~\ref{fig:framepack}): the context-only shift fades from 0.76 at 2\,s to 0.10,
while the embedding-only shift is small at first (0.08) and then \emph{grows} to 0.25 \ci{0.17}{0.36}, the rollout
relaxing toward a default that moves with the conditioning; the two routes add up.

\paragraph{A phenomenological recursion.} Model a chunk's mean statistic as
\begin{equation}
x_{k+1} = w_w\,\bar x_{\text{window}} + w_s\,x_{\text{sink}} + w_p\,\mu, \qquad w_w + w_s + w_p = 1,
\label{eq:rec}
\end{equation}
where the opening counts as a window chunk until it rolls out and $\mu$ is the prompt's default. Without a sink the
opening is eventually forgotten; with one, $\alpha_\infty = w_s/(w_s+w_p)$, so a small sink weight can keep a large
share if the prompt's pull is also small. Bit-exact replay of the key--value cache measures the weights directly, by
regenerating one chunk from identical noise after changing only the sink, the window or the text conditioning: $w_s =
0.053$ \ci{0.040}{0.066} for synthetic tone shifts (0.036 in a dose--response design), and $w_w = 0.76$ (synthetic)
to 0.90--0.99 (natural differences), in 10 development prompts $\times$ 2 seeds.
Fitted to 120-s curves (6 scenes $\times$ 2 versions), the recursion gives $w_s = 0.060$ \ci{0.021}{0.097} and $w_w = 0.86$, implying $w_p\approx0.08$.
We preregistered this prediction and measured the prompt's pull by swapping only the text conditioning of one chunk to
a lighting prompt with a different default (study 14): $w_p = 0.088$ \ci{0.045}{0.149} at 15\,s (Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}b, c),
proportional to the default difference, with no effect from a prompt of equal default. The three weights sum to 1.00
with the fitted $w_w$, but to 0.90 or 1.13 with the replay-measured ones, and $\alpha_\infty$ is 0.29--0.41 depending
on which $w_s$ is used. Without a sink the measured pull is smaller (0.035 at 15\,s), so $w_p$ is not a property of the
prompt alone.

The recursion is a first-order description that holds for about 20\,s. With a sink, the prompt's pull rises to 0.26
\ci{0.17}{0.36} at 30\,s (preregistered test of time invariance, $+0.16$ \ci{0.10}{0.24}); fitted weights depend on the
horizon; and persistence jumps abruptly between 31.5 and 34.5\,s ($+0.45$ \ci{0.36}{0.53} with a three-frame sink,
$+0.46$ with six) and again near 52\,s, in LongLive and the other Self Forcing checkpoints too (Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}b, c).
With re-rotated sinks it is replaced by a small decline ($-0.08$ \ci{-0.13}{-0.02}). Its timing matches the ``sink collapse'' that LoL~\cite{lol}
attributes to rotary-position periodicity, and the role of intrinsic rotary frequencies in video length
extrapolation~\cite{riflex,roundround}: the sink keeps its absolute position, and at certain distances the current
chunk attends to it strongly. It coincides with the return of the opening's dunes and sea in study 9. Much of what a
Self Forcing sink keeps at 60\,s is therefore positional: with in-distribution positions it keeps 0.16--0.23 whatever
its depth, below what the recursion implies, and no more scene content than no sink; the aliased sink keeps 0.61,
above it. The recursion does not explain either value.
