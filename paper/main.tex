\title{Drift to Default: Long Autoregressive Video Rollouts Relax\\ to a Prompt-Set State}
\author{Yilin Wang\\ University of Iowa}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Autoregressive video diffusion models generate long videos chunk by chunk, conditioned on their own output, and drift
over tens of seconds. We show that in models that read their past through attention, this drift is
\emph{relaxation}: the colour and texture statistics of a rollout move from those of its opening toward a default, and
the long-run state is an equilibrium,
\[
x_\infty \approx \alpha\, x_{\text{open}} + (1-\alpha)\,\mu.
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Generality.} We shift the brightness and colour of the same opening and measure how much survives. In 13
configurations of five open models (Self Forcing with DMD, SiD and GAN objectives, CausVid, LongLive, Rolling Forcing),
the share $\alpha$ is between 0.02 and 0.67 at 31\,s. The share depends strongly on the training objective even when
the architecture is identical.
\item \textbf{The default.} The default $\mu$ is where a fresh rollout of the same prompt ends ($r=0.93$ across
configurations). It moves with the lighting described in the prompt (16--45 \Lstar) and differs between models for the
same words.
\item \textbf{An exception that confirms the mechanism.} A diffusion-forcing model that continues by re-denoising 17
overlapping frames (SkyReels-V2) does not relax ($\alpha\approx1$), whatever its guidance, history noise or number of
steps. With only 5 overlapping frames it does ($-0.24$, preregistered).
\item \textbf{Mechanism.} An attention sink keeps a fixed share of the opening for at least 120\,s; without it the
opening is forgotten. A linear chunk-recursion model fitted to these curves recovers the sink's per-chunk weight that we
measure directly by bit-exact KV-cache replay ($\approx0.05$). A tiny weight still sets the equilibrium because the
prompt's per-chunk pull is equally small.
\item \textbf{Two levers.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Raise $\alpha$:} a training-free, content-aware correction increases $\alpha$ (+0.12), reduces drift, and
  keeps 83--93\% of legitimate brightness changes; post-hoc colour matching keeps 7--12\%.
  \item \emph{Move $\mu$:} a lighting description calibrated to the opening's measured brightness reduces drift by 26\%
  on Self Forcing; a free-form VLM caption does not.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
Every comparison is paired by seed on one GPU architecture. Eight studies were preregistered with hashed code, and we
report all their outcomes, including the three primary hypotheses or studies that failed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Causal (autoregressive) video diffusion models~\cite{causvid,selfforcing} make streaming, open-ended generation
possible and underlie interactive world models. Their known weakness is drift: over tens of seconds the picture
darkens, saturates, loses texture or collapses. Many recent methods target this failure, through rolling windows,
attention sinks, re-aligned positions, longer self-rollouts and test-time
correction~\cite{longlive,rollingforcing,deepforcing,pathwise,freqforcing}. They are usually evaluated with one sample
per prompt, an aggregate quality score and no uncertainty. What drift actually \emph{is} has not been measured.

We treat drift as a measurement problem and borrow the design tools of clinical research: paired seeds, controlled
interventions, held-out prompts, cluster-bootstrap intervals and preregistered analyses. Our central finding is
simple. A long rollout \emph{relaxes}: every low-level statistic moves from its opening value toward a default set by
the prompt, and a sink holds back a fixed share of the opening. This one picture explains several observations that
otherwise look unrelated:
\begin{itemize}
\item why sinks reduce drift;
\item why real camera frames ``drift more'' than generated ones;
\item why a test-time correction that copies the anchor's tone works but over-corrects at early denoising steps;
\item why describing the opening in the prompt fails while calibrating the lighting words succeeds.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A measurement protocol for long-video drift: self-referenced metrics, held and \emph{perturbed} openings, paired
seeds pinned to one GPU architecture, and preregistration. It also exposes three evaluation pitfalls:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item the GPU architecture changes bf16 outputs;
  \item resumed jobs silently break pairing;
  \item a standard image-quality metric is blind to drift.
  \end{itemize}
\item The relaxation law, $x_\infty \approx \alpha x_{\text{open}} + (1-\alpha)\mu_{\text{prompt}}$. We establish it
interventionally, by shifting the brightness and colour of the same opening, across 13 configurations of five models,
with an exception for diffusion forcing that points to the conditioning mechanism.
\item A quantitative mechanism. Long-horizon persistence curves and direct KV-cache interventions give consistent
per-chunk weights, which explains how a sink with a tiny weight sets the equilibrium.
\item Two practical levers matching the two terms of the law, both evaluated in preregistered tests against
baselines: a content-aware correction raises $\alpha$, and calibrated prompts move $\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
No model is trained. Everything is inference on public weights, so the protocol applies to any open autoregressive
video model.

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Autoregressive video diffusion.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Diffusion Forcing~\cite{diffusionforcing} trains with independent per-frame noise.
\item CausVid~\cite{causvid} distils a bidirectional teacher~\cite{wan} into a causal few-step student with
DMD~\cite{dmd}.
\item Self Forcing~\cite{selfforcing} trains on the model's own rollouts.
\item Long-video successors add rolling KV windows, attention sinks (as in streaming language
models~\cite{streamingllm}), RoPE re-alignment, LoRA-based extension, and joint rolling
denoising~\cite{longlive,rollingforcing,deepforcing}.
\item SkyReels-V2~\cite{skyreels} extends videos by diffusion forcing over overlapping segments.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Error accumulation and correction.} Meta-ARVDM~\cite{metaarvdm} analyses error accumulation theoretically.
Pathwise test-time correction~\cite{pathwise} and FreqForcing~\cite{freqforcing} correct drift at inference; our
correction is a deliberately minimal member of this family. It transfers only the DC tone at one early step, which
lets us study the step as a knob.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} VBench~\cite{vbench} scores imaging quality with MUSIQ~\cite{musiq}. We show that MUSIQ is
nearly blind to drift, and we measure drift relative to each prompt's own fresh-start distribution, in colour
statistics and in DINOv2~\cite{dinov2} features.

\paragraph{Held and perturbed openings.} To separate drift from what the prompt asks for, an opening image is held for
0.56\,s, encoded as the first chunk (or as the prefix video for SkyReels), and continued for 31\,s with a generic driving
prompt. Openings are NAVSIM~\cite{navsim} front-camera frames or first frames generated by the model. In the
interventional study, the same opening is shifted in CIELAB before encoding: \Lstar{} by $\pm$8 and $\pm$16, and
$b^*$ by $\pm$12.

\paragraph{Metrics.} For each video we record per-frame Lab means, saturation, \Lstar{} contrast and Laplacian
sharpness. The \emph{persistence} $\alpha$ is the within-scene slope of the end value (last 2\,s) on the opening value.
For prompt-only rollouts, drift is measured against 10 fresh 5-second generations of the same prompt: $D$ is the
Mahalanobis distance of colour statistics and $K$ the DINOv2 nearest-neighbour distance.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Seeds are paired across arms, and every arm of a scene runs in one job on one GPU
architecture. We use mixed models or seed-averaged paired differences, with scene-cluster bootstrap intervals and Holm
correction within each preregistered family. Each preregistration froze its code by SHA-256 hash before generation, and
the last four were committed publicly first.

\section{Drift is relaxation toward a prompt-set default}\label{sec:relax}
\paragraph{Observational evidence.} Across 40 held openings with a generic prompt, the change of every statistic over
31\,s is strongly anti-correlated with its opening value (Table~\ref{tab:attr}): $r$ between $-0.79$ and $-0.95$ for
\Lstar, $a^*$, $b^*$, saturation, sharpness and contrast, on both Self Forcing and LongLive. For brightness:
\[
\Delta L = 22.8 - 0.69\,L_{\text{open}}, \quad R^2 = 0.82.
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item Only 22--40\% of an opening's deviation survives.
\item Real and generated openings fall on the same line ($p=0.80$), so real frames ``drift more'' only because they are
brighter.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The default is set by the prompt.} Continuing the same openings under four lighting prompts gives four
levels (Fig.~\ref{fig:default}b). On Self Forcing these are sunny 43, overcast 34, dusk 19 and night 16 \Lstar; the
lighting term has $p\approx5\times10^{-78}$. On LongLive the same words give 45, 40, 32 and 21. The prompt sets the
target, and the mapping from words to brightness is model-specific.

\paragraph{Interventional evidence across models (preregistered).} Observational regressions compare different scenes,
whose content differs along with their brightness. We therefore shifted the opening \emph{within} each of 24 scenes (12
NAVSIM, 12 generated): \Lstar{} by $-16, -8, 0, +8, +16$ and $b^*$ by $\pm12$. Each was continued for 31\,s by 13
configurations (Table~\ref{tab:models}); every configuration of a scene ran in one job on one GPU architecture. The
persistence $\alpha$ is the within-scene slope of the end value on the opening value. Fig.~\ref{fig:relax}a shows the
results.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Every attention-based model relaxes.} $\alpha_L$ ranges from 0.02 (LongLive without a sink) to 0.67
(Self Forcing GAN); colour temperature behaves alike.
\item \textbf{The training objective matters even with everything else fixed.} Five Self Forcing checkpoints share
architecture, context and sampler, yet $\alpha_L$ ranges from 0.12 (SiD) to 0.67 (GAN) and the default from 17 to 47
\Lstar.
\item \textbf{The default is the end of a fresh rollout.} Across 10 configurations, the zero-drift level estimated from
the shifted openings correlates $r=0.93$ with where prompt-only fresh starts end (Fig.~\ref{fig:defaultfresh}). A model's
default can therefore be measured from a few cheap fresh rollouts.
\item \textbf{Diffusion forcing is the exception.} SkyReels-V2-DF keeps the opening: $\alpha_L = 1.09$ \ci{1.02}{1.16} at
10\,s and $0.96$ \ci{0.86}{1.07} at 20\,s (12 scenes). In an exploratory check, removing classifier-free guidance,
tripling the history noise, or cutting to 10 steps all left $\alpha\approx1$. Its continuation re-denoises 17
overlapping frames, a pixel-level constraint, whereas the KV-cache models only attend to their past. Weakening that
constraint makes SkyReels relax:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exploratory (4 scenes):} with 5 overlapping frames, $\alpha$ at 20\,s fell from 0.89 to 0.52; with 9
frames it was unchanged.
\item \textbf{Preregistered confirmation (study 8; 12 new scenes, both overlaps in one job per scene):} at 15--20\,s,
$\alpha = 0.77$ with 5 frames vs $1.01$ with 17. The difference is $-0.24$ \ci{-0.40}{-0.09}, $p=0.014$, and is
negative in 11 of 12 scenes. With 5 frames, persistence falls steadily from 1.09 at 5\,s to 0.71 at 20\,s.
\end{itemize}
The conditioning mechanism, not autoregression or diffusion forcing as such, decides whether a model relaxes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mechanism: an equilibrium between the anchor and the prompt}\label{sec:mech}
Model a chunk's mean statistic as a linear recursion:
\[
x_{k+1}-\mu = w_w\big(\bar x_{\text{window}}-\mu\big) + w_s\big(x_{\text{sink}}-\mu\big).
\]
The opening counts as a window chunk until it rolls out; after that, with a sink, it keeps a direct pull $w_s$. The
prompt's pull is $w_p = 1-w_w-w_s$. Without a sink the opening is eventually forgotten ($\alpha\to0$). With a sink the
equilibrium share is
\[
\alpha_\infty = \frac{w_s}{w_s + w_p}.
\]
The share is large whenever the prompt's pull is as weak as the sink's.

\paragraph{Two independent measurements agree.} Bit-exact KV-cache replay lets us shift only the sink or only the
window and regenerate the next chunk from identical noise. It measures $w_s = 0.053$ \ci{0.040}{0.066} for synthetic
tone shifts, $0.036$ in a dose-response design, and $w_w \approx 0.76$ (synthetic) to $0.90$--$0.99$ (natural
differences). Fitting the recursion to the long-horizon persistence curve alone gives:
\begin{itemize}
\item Self Forcing: $w_s = 0.049$ \ci{0.005}{0.069}, $w_w = 0.85$, $\alpha_\infty = 0.32$;
\item LongLive: $w_s = 0.054$, $w_w = 0.81$.
\end{itemize}
This resolves an earlier puzzle: the sink's direct effect on the next chunk is tiny, yet it roughly halves drift.


A preregistered factorial study of window and sink sizes (Appendix~\ref{sec:ctx}) already showed that a 3-frame sink cuts colour
drift by more than half on fresh rollouts; here we ask how it does so.

\paragraph{Interventional tests (preregistered, Holm over four).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{LongLive.} Removing the sink lowers $\alpha_L$ from 0.19 to 0.02 ($-0.17$ \ci{-0.28}{-0.06}, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.013$).
\item \textbf{Self Forcing, colour.} Removing the sink lowers $\alpha_b$ from 0.25 to $-0.01$ ($-0.26$
\ci{-0.34}{-0.18}, $p<0.001$).
\item \textbf{Self Forcing, brightness: not supported at 31\,s} ($-0.06$ \ci{-0.17}{0.05}). Its window forgets slowly
($w_w\approx0.9$), so at 31\,s the sink-less run had not yet forgotten. In the preregistered long-horizon subset (6
scenes, 120\,s), the sink holds 0.44--0.54 from 60\,s on, while without it persistence is $-0.10$ to $-0.07$. The
difference is $+0.56$ \ci{0.34}{0.75} at 118\,s.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Confirmation at 60\,s (preregistration 07).} We then tested the sink at 60\,s on all 24 scenes, for Self Forcing
and for CausVid (288 videos, Fig.~\ref{fig:relax}c).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Self Forcing:} with a sink, 0.58 of the opening remains; without one, 0.03. Difference $+0.55$ \ci{0.45}{0.65},
$p_{\text{Holm}} < 10^{-9}$.
\item \textbf{CausVid:} 0.23 vs 0.04, difference $+0.19$ \ci{0.14}{0.24}, $p_{\text{Holm}} < 10^{-6}$.
\item \textbf{Quantitative predictions.} Without a sink, Self Forcing's persistence fell from 0.24 at 30\,s to 0.03 at 60\,s,
as predicted. With a sink, the plateau of 0.58 lies just above the band the recursion predicted, \ci{0.25}{0.54}: the
model gets the shape right and the level slightly low.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Fitting the recursion.} Fitted to the 120-s curve, the model gives:
\begin{itemize}
\item Self Forcing: $w_s = 0.060$ \ci{0.021}{0.097}, $w_w = 0.86$, $\alpha_\infty = 0.42$ \ci{0.25}{0.54}. KV-cache
replay measured $0.053$ \ci{0.040}{0.066}.
\item LongLive: $w_s = 0.034$ \ci{0.027}{0.045}.
\item Ordering on the 31-s curves: the fitted sink weights follow the order of $\alpha$ (GAN 0.11, SiD and Rolling
Forcing 0.08, CausVid 0.05, LongLive 0.02).
\end{itemize}
Two caveats:
\begin{itemize}
\item From a 31-s curve alone, Self Forcing's sink weight is not identifiable, because the plateau is not reached yet.
\item Both long curves dip near 30\,s and then recover, which the first-order model does not capture. With six scenes
this may be noise.
\end{itemize}


\section{Two levers}\label{sec:levers}
\paragraph{Raising $\alpha$: a content-aware correction.} At denoising step 1 of 4, the noisy chunk is run once more
with a temporary KV cache that holds only the anchor. The difference between the per-channel spatial means (the DC tone)
of that prediction and of the real one is added back, scaled by $\beta$. Nothing else changes, including the main cache
and the random stream.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Confirmation (preregistered, 40 held-out prompts $\times$ 4 seeds).} Colour drift $D$ falls by 1.48
\ci{1.19}{1.76} at $\beta=1$ and by 1.78 at $\beta=1.5$. Structure drift $K$ is non-inferior. On held-out prompts with
legitimate brightness changes, 83\% / 93\% of the change is kept.
\item \textbf{Baselines.} Post-hoc colour matching to the anchor (mean, or Reinhard mean and standard deviation)
reaches similar $D$ ($-0.89$, $-1.58$) but keeps only 7\% / 12\% of legitimate transitions (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}).
\item \textbf{The step is a knob.} Correcting at step 0 removes more drift but erases tunnels (56\% kept); steps 2--3
do nothing.
\item \textbf{Cost and transfer.} The correction costs one forward pass per chunk and some flicker. On LongLive it
reduces $D$ by half as much.
\item \textbf{It works by raising $\alpha$.} In the relaxation study the correction raises Self Forcing's
persistence from 0.28 to 0.41 ($+0.12$ \ci{0.05}{0.20}, preregistered). In exploratory runs it does the same on
CausVid ($+0.09$ \ci{0.03}{0.14}), and on LongLive the estimate is $+0.06$ \ci{-0.06}{0.18}. It is therefore an
artificial strengthening of the sink's pull, restricted to the DC tone.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Moving $\mu$: calibrated prompts.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Free-form caption (preregistered).} Describing the opening with a Qwen2.5-VL~\cite{qwenvl} caption does
not reduce brightness drift on either model (Self Forcing $-1.21$ \ci{-3.09}{0.36}). The caption describes half of the
normally lit frames as ``dim'' and pulls them darker. It does improve content retention (+0.10 / +0.15).
\item \textbf{Calibrated lighting (preregistered).} Choosing the lighting sentence whose measured default is nearest
the opening's brightness reduces $|\Delta L|$ by 26\% on Self Forcing (Holm $p = 0.013$); with the correction, by 44\%.
\item \textbf{Calibration is per model.} On LongLive, the preregistered test was not significant even with LongLive's
own calibration; the lighting-only prompt reduces drift by 34\% (secondary).
\end{itemize}
(Appendix, Fig.~\ref{fig:prompts}).

\paragraph{The law predicts these outcomes out of sample.} Take $\alpha$ and the generic default from the relaxation
study (different scenes) and the lighting defaults from the lighting experiment (different openings). Then
$\Delta L \approx (1-\alpha)(\mu - L_{\text{open}})$ predicts the 31-s brightness change of every opening under the generic
and the calibrated prompt in the calibrated-lighting experiment:
\begin{itemize}
\item correlation 0.82 on Self Forcing and 0.77 on LongLive, mean absolute error 3.8 \Lstar;
\item the mean benefit of calibration is overpredicted ($-4.5$ predicted vs $-3.2$ and $-2.8$ observed).
\end{itemize}
The law is a usable forecast of how a prompt choice will change long-horizon brightness.




\section{Discussion: what this means for long-horizon world models}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Drift is a forgetting-and-reverting process, not accumulating noise.} In every attention-based model we
tested, a rollout forgets its opening at a model-specific rate and reverts to the default its prompt sets. The default
is cheap to measure from a few fresh rollouts.
\item \textbf{Two measurable numbers describe a model's long-horizon fidelity:} the share of the opening it keeps
($\alpha$) and its default ($\mu$). Both change with the training objective even when architecture and inference are
fixed. Reporting them would make long-video models comparable in a way that a single quality score is not.
\item \textbf{The levers follow from the law.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Raise $\alpha$: keep the opening as a sink, or transfer the anchor's tone at an early denoising step, which
  keeps legitimate changes because it acts only on the DC component.
  \item Move $\mu$: describe the lighting with words whose measured default matches the opening.
  \end{itemize}
A world model conditioned on a real camera frame and a caption will drift toward the caption unless the caption's
default matches the frame.
\item \textbf{Conditioning by overlap is different.} The diffusion-forcing model kept its opening, and weakening its
overlap constraint made it relax (preregistered). A hard pixel-level overlap therefore protects against reversion to
the default. It also passes errors forward unchanged. Whether overlap conditioning trades drift-to-default for
error accumulation is the most important open question here.
\end{itemize}

Three preregistered studies or primary tests failed, one quantitative prediction missed, and four earlier claims were
withdrawn; Appendix~\ref{app:failed} lists them all.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item All models but SkyReels share the Wan2.1-1.3B base, and only one diffusion-forcing model was tested.
\item Horizons are 31--120\,s; openings are held images, not real video, and are continued with one generic prompt in the
interventional studies.
\item The statistics are global colour and texture summaries. Structural failures (objects melting, geometry drift) are
not captured by $\alpha$.
\item The correction acts on colour only. Lighting calibration uses four discrete levels and cannot match openings
brighter than the sunny default.
\item One seed per opening version in the interventional studies; precision comes from scenes and versions instead.
\end{itemize}

\section{What did not hold}\label{app:failed}
We report claims that were revised or withdrawn after checks, and the preregistered predictions that failed.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Withdrawn claims:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item that MUSIQ rewards collapsed videos (it is merely insensitive);
  \item that the sink stabilises the default (trend difference $+0.5$ \ci{-2.4}{3.5});
  \item that the model follows its context by 75\% (only for artificial shifts);
  \item that early dynamics forecast the final brightness.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Failed preregistered tests:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item describing the opening with a free-form caption (study 3);
  \item calibrated lighting on LongLive (studies 4 and 5);
  \item the sink effect on Self Forcing brightness at 31\,s (study 6). It is supported at 60\,s in study 7, but that
  endpoint was chosen after seeing study 6.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{A failed quantitative prediction:} the recursion predicted a Self Forcing plateau of 0.25--0.54, and the
observed plateau was 0.58 (study 7).
\item \textbf{Unexplained:} a sink of one latent frame retained less of the opening than no sink (exploratory).
\end{itemize}


\section{Context strategies (preregistration 1)}\label{sec:ctx}
A preregistered factorial study (10 strategies, 40 prompts $\times$ 4 seeds) found:
\begin{itemize}
\item a 3-frame sink cuts colour drift by 7.56 \ci{6.81}{8.32};
\item window size matters only without a sink;
\item RoPE re-alignment, which Self Forcing was not trained for, does not help;
\item MUSIQ varies only between 66 and 69 across strategies whose drift differs threefold;
\item the GPU architecture changes outputs, so 70\% of nominally paired comparisons were not paired.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Robustness: linearity of the response.} Is the response to the opening shift linear? We compared $\alpha$
estimated from the $\pm8$ versions with $\alpha$ from the $\pm16$ versions, and the brighter half of the shifts
($0,+8,+16$) with the darker half.
\begin{itemize}
\item For 10 of 14 configurations, the $\pm8$ and $\pm16$ estimates differ by at most 0.09, with CIs including 0.
\item SiD and the sink-1 configuration give near-zero slopes for small shifts and positive slopes for large ones
($+0.29$, $+0.25$).
\item LongLive with a sink keeps a darkening better than a brightening (0.36 vs 0.04).
\end{itemize}
The linear law is a good first-order description, not an exact one.

\paragraph{Pitfalls for evaluating video models.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The GPU architecture changes the video.} With bf16, the same seed and code give bit-identical videos
within an architecture but different videos across A40, A100/A800 and L40S/RTX~6000 Ada. Paired designs must pin the
architecture.
\item \textbf{Resumed jobs can silently break pairing.} Generators that skip existing outputs resume a preempted job
wherever it lands.
\item \textbf{Popular quality metrics miss drift.} MUSIQ, used by VBench for imaging quality, barely moves across
strategies that differ threefold in colour drift.
\item \textbf{Natural variation is too small to estimate persistence.} Within a prompt, seeds start only about 2
\Lstar{} apart, so within-prompt slopes of end on start brightness have confidence intervals of $\pm0.4$. Controlled
shifts of $\pm16$ \Lstar{} are needed.
\item \textbf{Change-on-baseline regressions can mislead.} Regressing change on an opening value measured with error
produces spurious regression to the mean (Oldham's problem). Held openings are measured almost without error, and the
interventional design removes the issue.
\end{enumerate}
