\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
A finite-memory sampler that is ergodic under fixed conditioning forgets its initial state, so relaxation is expected;
we measure how much is kept and what decides it.
Across nine models the answer is whether the opening stays in the context and, for a sink at its absolute positions,
where it sits: the larger shares and returning scenes of such sinks are brief copies of the opening at the distances
where the sink is most attended.

A high $\alpha$ is not always better. Under a conflicting prompt (a snowy opening, a city prompt), forgetting the
opening is following the prompt, and an anchor that keeps the opening also passes its errors forward (SkyReels
amplifies the shift in 5 of 12 scenes). Persistence is better treated as a property to choose and to report at a stated
horizon, with the default and its offset. A world model conditioned on a frame and a description drifts toward the description's default. In Self Forcing the
mechanism that keeps the opening's scene is the unstable, positional one: keeping the opening's content but not
its errors is the open problem.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Seven of the nine models are Wan2.1-based (FramePack and MAGI-1 are not). Content relaxation was replicated on FramePack and MAGI-1, and its prompt dependence on MAGI-1,
but the decomposition rests on Wan-based models, and the world model collapses after 20--30\,s. Interventions shift global tone or select a scene class (CLIP and DINOv2 scores are coarse proxies for content); openings are held images; scene content is compared across openings rather than shifted within one; most configurations use one seed per scene (the main Self Forcing results replicate with more); with Holm correction across all \pend{125} primary tests, \pend{67} of the \pend{91}
supported ones remain significant; the correction was not compared with Pathwise or FreqForcing.
