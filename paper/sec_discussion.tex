\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Any autoregressive sampler with finite memory that is ergodic under a fixed conditioning will eventually forget its
initial state and settle into a conditioning-dependent stationary distribution, so that video rollouts relax is
expected. What we measure is \emph{how much} is kept and \emph{what} decides it, and three answers hold across the
models we tested. The long-run share depends on how the opening enters the computation: attention to a key--value
cache alone forgets it completely, while a permanent anchor (a sink, a per-segment context prefix, overlapping frames)
holds a share; over 20\,s these anchors keep similar amounts, except that a 17-frame overlap keeps nearly everything, and
a sink's long-run effect hinges on its rotary position. The stationary state is set by the conditioning, for tone and for scene content, although
models differ in which parts of the conditioning carry tone. And the stationary state of models trained on short clips
is offset from where the same prompt starts; long-rollout training removes the offset but not the forgetting.

For world models, this means that a model conditioned on a real frame and a description drifts toward the
description's default unless it re-conditions on the frame. Persistence at a stated horizon and the default with its
offset are cheap to measure with our protocol and would make long-video models comparable where a single quality score
does not. Anchors that keep the opening also pass its errors forward, and positional bursts can bring a forgotten
opening back; keeping the opening's content while forgetting its errors is the open problem.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Six of the seven models share the Wan2.1-1.3B architecture; FramePack, the only other base, ran
for 20\,s. The content study, the prompt dependence, the recursion and the decomposition rest on Wan-based models, and
the world model collapses after 20--30\,s. Interventions shift global tone or select a scene class (CLIP scores are a
coarse proxy for content); openings are held images; each configuration of a scene uses one seed; Holm correction is
applied within studies only; the correction was not compared with Pathwise test-time correction or FreqForcing.
