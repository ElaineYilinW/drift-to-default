\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Any autoregressive sampler with finite memory that is ergodic under a fixed conditioning will eventually forget its
initial state, so that video rollouts relax is expected; what we measure is how much is kept and what decides it.
Across nine models the answer is whether the opening stays in the context and, for a sink at its absolute positions,
where it sits: the larger shares and returning scenes of such sinks are brief copies of the opening at the distances
where the sink draws the most attention. The default is set by the conditioning, and models differ in which part of
the conditioning carries tone and which the scene.

A high $\alpha$ is not always better. Under a conflicting prompt (a snowy opening, a city prompt), forgetting the
opening is following the prompt, and an anchor that keeps the opening also passes its errors forward (SkyReels
amplifies the shift in 5 of 12 scenes). Persistence is better treated as a property to choose and to report at a stated
horizon, together with the default and its offset; our protocol measures both cheaply. For world models, a model conditioned on a real frame and a
description drifts toward the description's default unless it re-conditions on the frame. In Self Forcing the
mechanism that keeps the opening's scene is the unstable, positional one: keeping the opening's content while
forgetting its errors is the open problem.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Six of the nine models share the Wan2.1-1.3B architecture and a seventh is a student of its
14B sibling; FramePack and MAGI-1 have other bases. Content relaxation was replicated on FramePack, but the prompt dependence
and the decomposition rest on Wan-based models, and the world model collapses after 20--30\,s. Interventions shift global tone or select a scene class (CLIP scores are a
coarse proxy for content); openings are held images; scene content is compared across openings rather than shifted within one; most configurations use one seed per scene (the Self Forcing sink results on tone and content replicate with three more); with Holm correction across all \pend{101} primary tests, \pend{57} of the \pend{78}
supported ones remain significant; the correction was not compared with Pathwise test-time correction or FreqForcing.
