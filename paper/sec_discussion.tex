\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Any autoregressive sampler with finite memory that is ergodic under a fixed conditioning will eventually forget its
initial state and settle into a conditioning-dependent stationary distribution, so that video rollouts relax is
expected. What we measure is \emph{how much} is kept and \emph{what} decides it, and three answers hold across the
models we tested. The long-run share depends on how the opening enters the computation: once the opening leaves a
key--value window it is forgotten (in the Wan-based cache models at 1.3B and 14B parameters, and in MAGI-1), while an opening that stays in the
context (a sink, MAGI-1's full history, FramePack's per-section prefix) holds a share. Re-denoising overlapping frames
passes the opening forward and so delays forgetting by about 20\,s, but does not stop it. A sink at in-distribution
positions holds a small share of tone, about 0.2 whatever its depth, and less of the scene (0.08 more than none); the
larger shares and the returning scenes of sinks at their original positions are positional bursts. The stationary
state is set by the conditioning, for tone and for scene content, and models differ in which part of the conditioning
carries which attribute: FramePack's image embedding sets the scene but not its brightness. And the stationary state of
models trained on short clips is offset from where the same prompt starts; long-rollout training removes the offset but
not the forgetting.

For world models, this means that a model conditioned on a real frame and a description drifts toward the
description's default unless it re-conditions on the frame. Persistence at a stated horizon and the default with its
offset are cheap to measure with our protocol and would make long-video models comparable where a single quality score
does not. Anchors that keep the opening also pass its errors forward, and positional bursts can bring a forgotten
opening back. In Self Forcing the mechanism that keeps the opening's scene is exactly the unstable one: keeping the
opening's content while forgetting its errors is the open problem.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Six of the nine models share the Wan2.1-1.3B architecture and a seventh is a student of its
14B sibling; FramePack and MAGI-1 have other bases. Content relaxation was replicated on FramePack, but the prompt dependence, the recursion
and the decomposition rest on Wan-based models, and the world model collapses after 20--30\,s. Interventions shift global tone or select a scene class (CLIP scores are a
coarse proxy for content); openings are held images; most configurations use one seed per scene (the Self Forcing sink results on tone and content replicate with three more); Holm correction is
applied within studies only; the correction was not compared with Pathwise test-time correction or FreqForcing.
