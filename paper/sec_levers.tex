\section{Two levers}\label{sec:levers}
Equation~\ref{eq:law} suggests two ways to keep a long rollout near its opening without retraining: raise $\alpha$, or
move $\mu$ toward the opening.

\paragraph{Raising $\alpha$: a content-aware correction.} At denoising step 1 of 4, the noisy chunk is denoised once
more with a temporary cache that holds only the anchor (the opening chunk). The difference between the per-channel
spatial means of the two predictions (the DC tone) is added to the real prediction, scaled by $\beta$; the main cache
and the random stream are untouched. In a preregistered confirmation on 40 held-out prompts $\times$ 4 seeds
(study~2), colour drift $D$ fell by 1.48 \ci{1.19}{1.76} at $\beta=1$ and by 1.78 \ci{1.45}{2.11} at $\beta=1.5$, with
structure drift $K$ non-inferior, and in the relaxation study the correction raised Self Forcing's persistence from 0.28
to 0.40 ($+0.12$ \ci{0.05}{0.20}, preregistered). Table~\ref{tab:baselines} compares it with two baselines. Post-hoc
colour matching to the anchor reaches a similar $D$ but, by construction, removes legitimate global changes too: on 7
held-out prompts with real brightness transitions (tunnels, garages, clouds), matching keeps 7--12\% of the transition
and the correction a median of 83\% (per-prompt values from 0.58 to 1.51, so on some prompts it amplifies the change). A
deeper attention sink, as in Deep Forcing~\cite{deepforcing} (study 13, bit-identical pairing), is a strong alternative:
nine sink frames cut $D$ by more than the correction at $\beta=1$ ($-0.45$ \ci{-0.75}{-0.15}) with smaller boundary
jumps, but a sink pulls everything toward the opening, and in two of the seven transition prompts it erased most of a
legitimate change (7\% and 19\% kept, against 151\% and 147\% for the correction). The correction costs one extra
forward pass per chunk (about 25\% more compute) and more frame-to-frame jitter; it is the more conservative choice
when real changes matter.

\paragraph{Raising $\alpha$ without bursts: placing the sink.} The attention probe also says where to put a sink. Held
at distance 66 (below the freezing threshold, chosen from study 27 on one seed), Self Forcing's three-frame sink kept 0.45 \ci{0.40}{0.49} of the opening in the third minute on a new seed (study 31, preregistered):
twice the re-rotated sink's 0.22 ($+0.23$ \ci{0.15}{0.31}) and as much as the sink at its original positions (0.43),
without bursts (equivalent within $\pm0.05$) and with more motion than the original positions
($+1.10$ \ci{0.69}{1.57} grey levels), as much as the re-rotated sink. Distance 448 kept less ($+0.07$
\ci{-0.00}{0.15} over re-rotated, not supported), and in LongLive distance 66 keeps only 0.17 (study 28), so the
distance has to be chosen per model.

\paragraph{One rule, four models.} We therefore fixed one rule (study 37): among the distances calibrated on a few
scenes, take the one that keeps most of the opening over 50--60\,s while freezing at most 7\% of the videos. It picks 66
for Self Forcing and 448 for LongLive (from studies 27 and 28), and 664 for CausVid and 157 for Self Forcing GAN
(calibrated on 8 scenes; their own probes correlate 0.98 and 0.99 with Self Forcing's and have the same peaks). On 16
openings never used in a sink study and a new seed (study 38, 120\,s, preregistered), the held sink kept more of the
opening over 60--120\,s than the re-aligned sink in all four models ($+0.23$ \ci{0.12}{0.34}, $+0.21$ \ci{0.07}{0.36},
$+0.20$ \ci{0.07}{0.29}, $+0.30$ \ci{0.18}{0.42}; Holm-supported except LongLive, $p=0.057$), close to the sink at its
original positions (0.41 vs 0.43, 0.30 vs 0.22, 0.24 vs 0.20; GAN 0.49 vs 0.66), with at most 3\% of the videos
frozen against up to 22\% at the original positions. The absence of bursts was shown in CausVid and GAN; in Self
Forcing and LongLive the estimates are near zero but the equivalence tests missed ($p=0.057$). The held sink moved no
less than the re-aligned one in CausVid (supported) and, by estimate, in Self Forcing and GAN, but less in LongLive
($-1.3$ grey levels). As a setting for general long-video generation it is weaker (study 39, preregistered: 40 prompts,
no held opening, 60\,s, VBench-style scores per 10-s segment). It cut the colour offset against the re-aligned sink in
Self Forcing (10.3 to 6.0~\Lstar) and GAN (8.8 to 4.3) but not in LongLive or CausVid, reduced the loss of imaging
quality in no model (GAN lost more, $-8.1$ \ci{-12.4}{-3.6}), and moved less (RAFT flow 5--14 lower). The sink at its
original positions scored as well on these summaries, and Rolling Forcing, trained on longer rollouts, best (colour
offset 2.9~\Lstar). A held sink is therefore a training-free control of how much of the opening is kept, not a quality
improvement.

\paragraph{Moving $\mu$: prompts.} Since the prompt sets the default, lighting words can be chosen so that the default
matches the opening. Choosing the lighting sentence whose \emph{measured} default is nearest the opening's brightness
reduces $|\Delta L|$ at 31\,s by 26\% on Self Forcing ($-2.45$ \ci{-4.08}{-1.04}, Holm $p=0.013$, study 4). A free-form
Qwen2.5-VL~\cite{qwenvl} caption of the opening does not reduce brightness drift (study 3), and the other five preregistered prompt tests failed, including LongLive with its own calibration
(study 5). The mapping from words to brightness is model-specific;
Appendix~\ref{app:default} shows how well Eq.~\ref{eq:law} forecasts these outcomes.
